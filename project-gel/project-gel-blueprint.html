02

Design pillars

The blob is alive

The hero never holds still. It breathes, jiggles, squashes when it stops and stretches when it runs. Every attack is made of its own goo and visibly leaves its body.

One thumb

Drag anywhere to move. Everything else is automatic. A full run has to be playable one-handed on a phone held upright.

Readable chaos

Glossy cyan hero, matte orange horde, muted blue floor. With 400 enemies on screen the player still finds the hero instantly.

Short runs, long growth

Five-minute chapters that end in a boss. Permanent upgrades between them make the next attempt stronger.

Core loop and run structure

Pick chapter→Survive waves→Collect XP gems→Level up: pick 1 of 3→Elites drop chests→Boss at 5:00→Coins→Permanent upgrades↺

One chapter Proposed

0:45Sprinters join.

4:00Elite #2.

5:00The boss arrives and normal spawns drop to 1 per second. The chapter ends when the boss dies.

Run rules

  • Skill slots: 8, any mix of attack and passive skills. Verify
  • Max skill level: 5. Guides show at least level 4. Verify
  • XP to the next level: 5 + 4L + 0.25L². About 20 level-ups in a full chapter, enough for roughly half of the 40 possible skill levels, so choices matter. Proposed
  • Level-up offers: 3 cards. Once 4 or more slots are filled, offers lean toward skills you already own (60% owned, 40% new), and never offer a new skill when all slots are full. Proposed
  • Death: one revive per run: full heal, 1 s of invulnerability, nearby enemies pushed back 4 m. The original gates revives behind an optional video. Sourced
  • Difficulty across chapters: enemy HP ×1.15 and damage ×1.08 per chapter. Within a run, enemy HP rises 12% per minute. Proposed
  • Chapter count: 100+, built from 6 arenas and difficulty tiers. Every 5th chapter is a double boss. Proposed
04

Gameplay handling

All values are Proposed starting points for tuning. One world unit is one meter, and the hero is 1.6 m tall.

Controls

DeviceInput
PhoneFloating joystick. Touching anywhere below the top bar sets the stick's center. 8 dp dead zone; speed ramps from zero to full between 8 and 48 dp; the knob stops at 64 dp. Lifting the finger stops the hero. The ring shows at 40% opacity under the thumb.
DesktopWASD or arrow keys, with diagonals normalized. Click and drag behaves like the phone stick.
GamepadLeft stick with a 0.15 radial dead zone.
AttackingNo button. Every skill fires on its own.
PausingTop-right button, Esc, or the app losing focus.

Movement

PropertyValueWhy
Top speed4.5 m/sAbout 2.8 body lengths per second: faster than every enemy except Sprinters.
Reach top speed0.08 sSnappy. Phone players feel any lag in a drag.
Stop from top speed0.06 sLifting the thumb has to stop the hero at once.
Turn rate900°/sFaces the new direction within a tenth of a second.
Lean into movement10°Sells speed. The jelly springs add lag on top.
Hurtbox radius0.42 mThe visible body is about 0.55 m, so near-misses feel fair.
Gem pickup radius1.6 mGems fly in at 4 m/s and accelerate at 40 m/s².

Crowds and damage

  • Enemies never fully block the hero. Each overlapping enemy slows the hero by 6%, up to 45%. Getting surrounded is dangerous because you slow down, not because you get stuck.
  • The hero pushes enemies aside (hero mass 10, enemy mass 1). Enemies push apart from each other using a spatial-hash grid.
  • Contact damage: each enemy can hurt you at most once every 0.5 s. There's no general invulnerability after a hit, only 0.4 s after closing the level-up screen and 1 s after a revive.

Targeting rules

RulePicks
NearestThe closest enemy within the skill's range.
RandomA random enemy that is on screen.
ClusterThe center of the busiest 2 × 2 m grid cell within range.

Camera

  • Perspective, tilted 52° down. It never rotates.
  • Shows 11 m of width at the hero's depth on a portrait phone, 19 m in landscape. The field of view is calculated from those widths.
  • Follows with a critically damped spring (0.12 s) and leads 0.8 m in the direction of movement.
  • Shake: 0.08 m for 0.15 s when the hero is hit; 0.25 m for 0.3 s on boss slams; never on kills. A setting turns it off.

Feedback

Bosses Proposed

One boss per chapter, rotating; every 5th chapter brings two at once. Chapter 1 boss HP is 2,500, ×1.2 per chapter. Bosses use the same mesh pipeline as enemies, scaled 2.5 to 3.5×, each with one silhouette feature so it reads at a glance. All attacks are telegraphed with red decals on the floor.

Big Stomper

A giant stickman in a helmet. Ground slam: 3 m red circle, 1.2 s warning, 40 damage. Every 10 s it calls a ring of 8 stickmen.

Hurler

Wears a boulder backpack and keeps 7 m away. Throws 3 boulders at once onto red target circles (1.0 s warning, 25 damage, 1.5 m radius). Jumps away when you get close.

Charger

Horned. Shows a red lane for 0.8 s, then charges along it at 14 m/s, three times in a row. Then it sits dizzy for 2.5 s and takes 50% extra damage.

Twins

Two stickmen joined by a glowing rope that hurts on touch (15 damage). Killing one enrages the other (+40% speed).

Conductor

Holds a baton and stands in the middle of the arena, raising spiral waves of enemies and firing slow rings of orange orbs with gaps to slip through.

09

3D art direction

Reading the reference image

  • Hero: glossy, saturated cyan with vinyl-toy proportions: a big torso, short thick legs, and a head that reads as a bump on the torso rather than a separate ball.
  • Hands: chunky fists with a violet-blue gradient and white glow outlines. White swoosh arcs trace their path.
  • Enemies: matte orange stickmen with round heads and long soft shadows, about 70% of the hero's height.
  • Floor: big beveled slate-blue stones, low contrast, darkening into navy at the edges.

Materials

Hero goo

  • Cyan body shading to violet toward the fists (a hint at the feet), driven by distance to those parts.
  • Wrapped diffuse lighting so shadows stay soft and colorful.
  • Fake subsurface glow: thin parts (limbs, head edge) brighten.
  • Pale cyan rim light, strength 0.55.
  • A tight white highlight plus a broad soft sheen. No textures needed.

Skill goo

  • The hero shader on projectile meshes, with a brighter core and less subsurface.
  • Cyan for light, fast skills; violet for heavy ones; hot pink when armed or charged.

Enemies

  • Three-band toon shading (lit #F5891F, mid #DA7016, shadow #B9580F) with soft band edges.
  • Faint warm rim, no specular highlight. Matte by design.

Floor

  • One beveled stone tile mesh, instanced in offset rows, with ±6% brightness and ±2° rotation per tile.
  • Darkened tile edges baked in. The arena center is lit and the edges fall off into navy.

Effects

  • Glowing ribbons for swooshes and trails, white core fading to cyan.
  • Floor decals for splats, scorch marks and red danger warnings.
  • A 2 cm white outline only on fists and projectiles, as in the key art.

Skill icons

  • 3D renders of the actual goo object in the real materials, seen from three-quarters above, on a rounded navy tile.
  • Cyan frame for attack skills, gold frame for passives.

Lighting, camera and post-processing

  • Key light: one directional light from the upper left, 55° high, neutral white. Sky fill #3A6A8F, ground fill #0A1F33.
  • Shadows: the hero casts a real soft shadow. Enemies use instanced soft shadow blobs stretched away from the light, which matches the long shadows in the reference and costs almost nothing.
  • Post-processing, in order: bloom (threshold 0.85, strength 0.6, so only effects glow), color grade (shadows lifted toward navy, saturation +8%), vignette 0.35, anti-aliasing. The low preset drops bloom.

Animation rules

  • The hero has no keyframed animation. Its run cycle is generated from speed (0.9 m stride, 5 cm bob) with springy follow-through on every part.
  • Squash on stop (height 88% for 0.12 s), stretch on start, breathing at idle (once every 4 s), a jolt when hit.
  • Volume stays constant: when a part squashes to height s, its width scales by 1/√s.
  • Enemies play a baked 12-frame run cycle at a rate matched to their speed, each starting at a random point so crowds don't march in step.
10

Blob technology

This is the section the whole look depends on. The hero has to move like real goo, and enemies have to look like one connected body instead of shapes piled on each other.

NeedTechnique
Hero: dynamic, jiggly, able to stretch, split and mergeSigned distance field (SDF) character: 15 shapes blended with a smooth minimum, posed by a procedural rig with spring physics, drawn by a custom shader (raymarching) inside the hero's on-screen bounding box. It writes depth so it sorts correctly with the 3D world.
Skill goo leaving the bodyWhile attached, extra spheres grow inside the hero's distance field. At the moment of release they hand off to a normal mesh projectile of the same size and position, with a wobble shader.
Enemies: connected-looking, hundreds at onceOne smooth mesh per enemy type, built by turning a smooth-blended distance field into a mesh. Rigged to a 10-bone skeleton and animated on the GPU from a baked animation texture: one draw call per enemy type.
Puddles (Aura, mine bases, splats)Flat floor quads whose shader blends 2D circles smoothly, so overlapping puddles merge into one shape. With Aura active, the hero's field gains a flat floor primitive so the body visibly melts into the puddle.

  • Springs: every part follows its animated position through a damped spring (stiffness 220, damping ratio 0.45), which gives overshoot and jiggle. The head and fists use stiffness 140 for more lag.
  • Living surface: a slow 3D ripple with 8 mm amplitude is always on, so the blob never looks frozen.
  • Hits: damage kicks the springs, so the whole body wobbles from the side it was hit.
  • Budget: at most 64 raymarch steps per pixel, early exit outside a bounding capsule, under 3 ms of GPU time on a mid-range phone.

Enemy pipeline

  1. Describe the stickman with the same rig format as the hero (capsules and spheres), blend radius 0.06 m.
  2. Turn it into a mesh in its rest pose at 64³ resolution, then reduce to about 800 triangles (close) and 300 (far).
  3. Weight each vertex to its two nearest bones, smoothed, so joints bend softly instead of pinching.
  4. Author run, attack, hit and death animations (procedurally or in Blender) and bake them into a bone-matrix texture.
  5. Draw all enemies of a type as one instanced mesh. Each instance carries its animation time, tint, hit-flash amount and scale. 400 stickmen cost one draw call.

An artist-friendly route to the same result: model with metaballs in Blender, convert and remesh, rig, then run the same bake.

Menus and UI

UI kit

  • Shapes: rounded rectangles (20 to 28 dp corners), a darker 6 dp lip under every button, and a soft highlight along the top edge, so buttons look like jelly.
  • Press: squash to 94% height and 104% width in 90 ms, then spring back with one wobble.
  • Type: Fredoka 600 to 700 for titles, numbers and buttons. White text on colored buttons gets a 3 dp navy outline.
  • Color roles: cyan for primary actions, violet secondary, gold for currency, red for danger, navy panels with slate borders.
  • Layout: portrait 390 × 844 as the reference size, safe areas respected, tap targets at least 48 dp, the main action always in the bottom third where the thumb is.
